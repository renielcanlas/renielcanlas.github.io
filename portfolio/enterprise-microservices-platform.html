Enterprise Microservices Platform

A scalable enterprise platform designed around Spring Boot microservices and a React microfrontend architecture.

Overview

Owned the end-to-end solution architecture for a distributed enterprise platform supporting high-volume transactions. The design focused on clear service boundaries, independent delivery, resilient integrations, and a frontend structure that allowed teams to work autonomously.

JavaSpring BootReactAzureMicroservices

Key contributions

  • Defined enterprise architecture standards, design principles, and reference architectures.
  • Led modernization and migration from legacy systems to cloud-native services.
  • Established CI/CD architecture with security, vulnerability management, and compliance controls.
  • Conducted architecture reviews and design governance across engineering teams.
  • Translated complex business requirements into scalable solutions for distributed systems.

Architecture focus

Service boundaries

The platform was shaped around business capabilities so services could evolve independently while retaining clear ownership. This made it easier to reason about data responsibilities, integration contracts, and the blast radius of change.

Frontend autonomy

A microfrontend approach allowed frontend work to be organized into independently deliverable areas. Shared standards and integration conventions kept the user experience coherent while enabling multiple teams to contribute in parallel.

Cloud-native operations

Resiliency, observability, and deployment concerns were treated as architecture concerns. The goal was not only to build services that functioned correctly, but to make their runtime behavior visible and manageable.

Delivery and governance

Architecture reviews provided a practical feedback loop between long-term technical direction and day-to-day delivery. Reference patterns gave teams a starting point for recurring decisions, while CI/CD controls integrated quality, security, and compliance checks into the delivery lifecycle.

Close collaboration with engineering and product stakeholders ensured solution decisions remained connected to business needs, delivery constraints, and the platform's future evolution.

What this enabled

  • A common technical direction for services, integrations, and frontend modules.
  • More consistent implementation decisions across teams and delivery streams.
  • A sustainable path for modernizing legacy capabilities.
  • Security and operational controls built into delivery instead of added at the end.
